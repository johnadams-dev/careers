What Happens to Your MLS Access When You Switch Brokerages in Seminole County?

Home›Seminole County Brokerages›Switching and MLS Access

Updated September 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

Your MLS access follows your association membership, which follows your brokerage's office location rather than your home address. Seminole County has no association of its own, so its agents are on Stellar MLS through the regional board, and a move between two Seminole offices is usually a change of brokerage rather than of platform. Moving across a county line is where that stops being automatic. Your listings belong to the brokerage rather than to you, so what happens to active and pending transactions is a matter for the two brokers and the seller. The transfer sequence routinely takes more than a week, during which you may be unable to open a lockbox.

Your MLS access does not simply travel with you

People assume the MLS subscription is theirs, the way a professional certification is. It is not quite. Your access is tied to your membership, your membership is tied to the association your office sits in, and the day your license moves, that chain is briefly broken.

Nothing about that is unusual or sinister. It is simply a sequence, and the people who get hurt by it are the ones who did not know there was one.

Seminole is simpler than most, and that is the trap

Here is where this county differs from the one next door. Volusia County is split across three separate REALTOR boards, and Flagler has a fourth, so an agent moving offices there can genuinely change association and system. Seminole has no association of its own at all. Its agents join the Orlando Regional REALTOR Association, and the MLS is Stellar MLS.

So a move between two Seminole brokerages is usually a change of brokerage rather than a change of platform. Your listings move, your access is re-pointed, and the tool you open in the morning is the same one.

The trap is the assumption that this holds at the county line. It does not automatically. Moving to an office in another county can mean a different association, and whether it also means a different MLS depends entirely on which one. Conveniently for anyone looking west: the West Volusia Association of REALTORS, covering the DeLand, DeBary and Deltona side of Volusia, is also on Stellar MLS. Other boards in the region are not.

Your office location decides it, not your address

Worth repeating because it is the root of most confusion. Your association and therefore your MLS follow the location of the brokerage office your license is registered to. Not where you live, not where you sell. Two agents on the same street can be in different associations because their offices are.

Which means that when you are comparing two offers from firms in different places, you are also comparing associations, systems and dues structures, and nobody in that conversation is likely to raise it unless you do.

What actually happens to your listings

This is the part that surprises experienced agents, and it is worth being blunt. Your listings belong to the brokerage, not to you. The listing agreement is between the seller and the firm, and your name on it does not change that.

When you leave, what happens to an active listing is a matter for the two brokers and the seller, not something you can decide unilaterally. Some firms will release a listing to follow you if the seller wants that. Others will not. Pending transactions are handled separately again, and how a firm treats them on the way out is one of the more revealing things you can learn about it.

Ask the question before you sign with anyone, while you are a prospective asset rather than a departing one. The answer you get then is the honest one.

The gap, and how long it really lasts

The sequence when you move: your license registration transfers to the new employing broker, your association membership is updated to the new office, your MLS access is re-pointed, and your lockbox credential is reissued or re-associated.

None of those steps is long. Together, with a weekend in the middle and a form that needed correcting, they routinely add up to more than a week. During that window you may be unable to open a lockbox or enter a listing, and if you have a transaction mid-flight that is a real operational problem rather than an inconvenience.

Two habits fix it. Time the move for a gap in your pipeline rather than the middle of a busy month, and ask the incoming broker exactly who submits which form and when, so nothing sits waiting on someone's desk because each of you assumed the other had it.

What about the dues you already paid?

Assume they do not follow you cleanly and be pleasantly surprised. Association dues, the MLS subscription and the state and national REALTOR dues are billed on different calendars by different bodies, and a mid-cycle move can leave you having paid one body while owing another.

If you are moving within Seminole, where the association does not change, this is usually straightforward. If you are moving across a county line, ask specifically what transfers, what is refundable and what you will be asked to pay again. Get it before you sign, not after.

What the credential actually is, and why reissuing takes time

Four separate things carry your professional identity, and they are held by four different bodies: your license by the state, your association membership by the local board, your MLS subscription by Stellar MLS, and your lockbox credential by the association again.

Each has to be updated when you move, and none of them update because another one did. That is the whole reason a transfer that sounds like a single form takes over a week: it is four processes in sequence, each waiting on the one before, and any of the four can stall on a detail.

For scale on how often this happens here: Seminole County holds 4,254 active sales associates across 697 registered firms, and 324 new licenses were issued in the county in the last twelve months. With 1,058 active brokers and no single firm holding more than about 4.6 percent of the county's agents, movement between firms is constant rather than exceptional.

It is also why the four recurring costs renew on four different calendars, and why a mid-cycle move is the moment that misalignment becomes visible.

The questions to ask before you accept

Four, and they take two minutes.

  1. Which association and which MLS will I be in from your office, and is that the same one I am in now?
  2. What happens to my active listings and my pending transactions?
  3. Who submits the transfer paperwork, and what is the realistic gap before I can open a lockbox again?
  4. What will I be asked to pay again, and what carries over?

A firm that answers all four immediately has moved agents before and knows how. A firm that cannot is telling you something about what your first two weeks there will feel like.

The timing, in days rather than adjectives

People ask how long a move takes and get told "about a week", which is true and useless. The useful version is where the days actually go.

  • License registration to the new employing broker. Fast once submitted, and the step everything else waits on.
  • Association membership updated to the new office. A form and a review.
  • MLS access re-pointed. If you are moving inside Seminole this is a change of brokerage on an existing subscription rather than a new one, which is the easy case.
  • Lockbox credential reissued, quoted at around 48 business hours for the electronic key on a new application.

Four steps, none long, all sequential, with at least one weekend in the middle of any real-world example. That is how a set of same-day tasks becomes 7 to 10 days, and why timing a move into a quiet stretch of your pipeline is worth more than any concession you could negotiate.

Why this matters more here than the numbers suggest

Seminole County holds 4,254 active sales associates across 697 registered firms, and 38.9% of its sales associate licenses are inactive, the highest rate of any sizable county in Florida. In a market where that many people stop, a badly handled move at the wrong moment is not a minor administrative irritation. It is a month of lost production for someone who may not have a month of cushion.
