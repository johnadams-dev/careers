The association, and the thing that makes Seminole unusual

Seminole County has no REALTOR association of its own. That is genuinely unusual for a county holding 4,254 active sales associates, and it is the first thing to understand.

Volusia County next door has three separate boards and Flagler has its own. Seminole's agents instead belong to a large regional association headquartered in Orlando covering seven counties. Membership therefore feels different here: it is a regional body rather than a local board where everyone knows everyone.

The MLS, and why the county line is the thing to check

Because one association covers the whole county, a move between two Seminole brokerages is usually a change of firm rather than a change of platform. That is simpler than the situation in Volusia.

The catch is at the boundary. Moving to an office in another county can mean a different association, and whether that also means a different MLS depends entirely on which one. the West Volusia Association of REALTORS, which covers the DeLand, DeBary and Deltona side of Volusia, is on Stellar MLS as well, so that particular move does not change your platform. Other boards in the region are on different systems.

Signs: eight jurisdictions, and what this page will not tell you

Sign placement is governed by eight separate sets of rules inside this county: the county's own code for unincorporated areas plus seven incorporated cities.

Three things hold everywhere. Signs may not be placed in the public right-of-way, which extends several feet past the pavement and often past the sidewalk. Signs may not be attached to utility poles, trees or traffic control devices. And inside a deed-restricted community the recorded covenants can be stricter: under F.S. 720.304(6) Florida protects only a security contractor's sign within ten feet of an entrance, with no equivalent protection for a for sale sign.

This page deliberately does not publish permitted dimensions, setbacks or time limits per city. Those differ and they are amended often enough that a figure printed here would be unreliable, and a wrong sign rule gets an agent cited rather than embarrassed. Get current figures from your association's Governmental Affairs Department or the city planning department, and ask four things: maximum sign area, setback from the right-of-way, whether a permit is required, and how soon after closing the sign must come down.

What joining actually costs

There is no single number, which is the part recruiting conversations most often understate. Four separate charges, billed by different bodies on different renewal calendars: local association dues, state and national REALTOR dues, the MLS subscription, and lockbox or key service.

Ask any firm for all four in writing with the renewal month for each. The second year is when the total surprises people, because the cycles do not align and nothing arrives together.

The county in numbers

Counted from Florida's weekly license file on September 22, 2026: 4,254 active sales associates, 1,058 active brokers, 407 broker associates, 697 registered brokerage firms and 161 branch offices. A further 2,711 sales associate licenses are inactive, 38.9% of the county total and the highest rate of any sizable county in Florida. For comparison, Volusia County holds 4,816 active agents at an inactive rate of 33.9%.

The first week, in order

If you are starting here, this is the sequence, and the order matters because each step waits on the one before it.

  1. License registered under your employing broker. Until this happens nothing else can.
  2. Association membership established at the office location, which determines everything downstream.
  3. MLS subscription activated, with the 60-day clock on the three required classes starting here rather than whenever you get to them.
  4. Lockbox credential applied for, quoted at around undefined for the electronic key.
  5. All four renewal dates written into a calendar the week you join. Ten minutes, and it is the cheapest professional habit in this business.

Stacked together with a weekend in the middle, steps one to four commonly take more than a week. Agents who book a first showing for the weekend after they join find this out on the Thursday.

The five things people get wrong

Collected from the rest of this site, because they recur.

  • Your association follows the office, not your home. Two agents on one street can be in different associations.
  • Your broker cannot fix a lockbox credential. Neither can the MLS help desk. Only the association can.
  • A mailing address is not a jurisdiction. A property with a Sanford or Oviedo postal address can sit in unincorporated Seminole County under the county's rules.
  • Your listings belong to the brokerage. What happens to active and pending deals when you leave is for the brokers and the seller to settle, not you.
  • A quoted joining cost is almost never all four charges. Ask for every line item with its renewal month.

How current this is, and what is not covered

The licensee figures are counted from the state file on the date above and are re-pulled periodically. The association, MLS and lockbox details were checked against published sources on the same date.

One more note on scope. This page covers the operational mechanics of holding and using a license in Seminole County. It does not cover the state-level rules that apply identically everywhere in Florida, such as the 63-hour pre-license course, the 45 hours of post-licensing education owed before your first renewal, or what must appear on advertising. Those do not vary by county and are covered elsewhere on this site.

Not covered here, deliberately: per-city sign dimensions, current dues amounts, and anything else that changes faster than a page can. For those the answer is a phone call, and the association's Governmental Affairs Department is the right number.
