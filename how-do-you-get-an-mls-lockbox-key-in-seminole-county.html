How Do You Get an MLS Lockbox Key in Seminole County?

Updated September 2026 · Reviewed by Adams, Cameron & Co.

Quick answer

Your REALTOR association issues the lockbox credential, not your brokerage and not the MLS. In Seminole County that means the Orlando Regional REALTOR Association, because the county has no association of its own. The association provides access to both SentriLock and Supra; the SentriLock electronic key runs on your phone and is quoted at around 48 business hours to process. The sequence is license activation, then association and MLS membership, then the key application, and stacked together those routinely take more than a week, which is the gap that catches new agents with a showing booked.

Who actually issues the key

Not your brokerage, and not Stellar MLS. Your REALTOR association issues your lockbox credential, and in Seminole County that means the Orlando Regional REALTOR Association, because the county has no board of its own.

That distinction matters the first time something goes wrong. If your key stops working, your broker cannot fix it and the MLS help desk cannot fix it. The association can.

The two systems, and why this county has both

the Orlando Regional REALTOR Association gives members access to SentriLock and to Supra. Most markets standardize on one. Having both available is a consequence of covering seven counties that did not all arrive at the same answer historically.

SentriLock runs as an electronic key on your phone, applied for by form, with processing quoted at around 48 business hours. The association keeps SentriLock specialists on site to help members set it up, which is worth using rather than fighting the app alone.

The practical question to ask your broker on day one is not which system is better. It is which system the listings you will actually be showing are on, because that is what decides whether you can open a door.

The timing problem that catches almost everyone

Here is the sequence, and the gap is where people get hurt:

  1. Your license is activated under an employing broker.
  2. You join the association and become an MLS subscriber.
  3. You apply for a lockbox credential.
  4. Processing takes its stated time, quoted at around 48 business hours for the electronic key.

None of those steps is long. Stacked end to end, with a weekend in the middle and a form that needed correcting, they routinely add up to more than a week. New agents who set a first showing for the weekend after they join discover this on the Thursday.

If you are in that gap, the answer is not to improvise. Ask a colleague with an active key to accompany you. Showing a property you cannot lawfully access is not a paperwork problem, it is an access problem, and it is your license that carries the consequence.

The rules that carry real penalties

Every lockbox system logs every opening, with your credential and a timestamp. That record is the point of the system and it is the thing people forget.

  • Never share your key or your credential. Not with an unlicensed assistant, not with a contractor, not with a buyer waiting at the door. The log will show your name.
  • Never give out a lockbox code. The code exists so that you are physically present, which is the whole security model.
  • Do not let anyone into a property who is not accompanied by you. Inspectors, photographers and appraisers are scheduled through the listing agent for this reason.

Violations are handled by the association rather than informally, and because the log is objective there is rarely much to discuss after the fact.

Buying a used lockbox from another agent

Agents leaving the business sell their boxes, and it usually looks like a bargain. Before you buy one, confirm it is the same system your association issues, confirm it can be reassigned out of the previous owner's account, and confirm the previous owner's membership is in good standing. A box that cannot be transferred is a paperweight, and the seller has already left the business.

What this costs, and the failure it causes

Lockbox or key service is the fourth of the four recurring costs of holding a working license here, alongside local association dues, state and national REALTOR dues, and the MLS subscription. It renews on its own calendar, which is precisely why it is the one people miss.

The failure mode is predictable: the renewal lapses quietly, the key stops working, and the agent finds out standing outside a listing with clients in the car. Put all four renewal dates in a calendar the week you join. It takes ten minutes and it is the cheapest professional habit in this business.

When the key fails at the door

It will happen, usually with clients standing behind you, and the difference between a professional and an amateur in that moment is entirely preparation.

The common causes are mundane. A phone battery flat enough that the electronic key will not authorize. No mobile signal at a property, which happens more often than you would expect on the rural edges of the county. A credential that lapsed at a renewal you did not notice. A box that was never actually placed, or was placed somewhere the listing notes do not mention.

What to do, in order: call the listing agent, because half the time the answer is that the box is on the side gate rather than the front door. Call your association if the credential itself is refusing, because they are the only ones who can act on it. And carry a charger, because the most frequent cause of an electronic key failing is the least interesting one.

What not to do: ask the listing agent to read you the code over the phone. They should refuse, you should not ask, and the log exists precisely to make that conversation unnecessary.

How many people this applies to here

For scale: Seminole County holds 4,254 active sales associates and 1,058 active brokers, spread across 697 registered firms, and 324 new licenses were issued in the county in the last twelve months. Every one of those new licensees goes through the sequence above, and most of them are surprised by it.

The county's agent population is also spread rather than concentrated. Longwood holds 931, Oviedo 853, Sanford 800, Lake Mary 746, Altamonte Springs 606, Winter Springs 570 and Casselberry 314. Because no city dominates, you will be showing across the county rather than within one town, which is exactly the situation where having the wrong lockbox system costs you a showing.

What is your next step?

Before your first showing, confirm three things with your broker: which lockbox system the office is on, who submits your application, and what you are expected to do in the days before the credential is live. All three are ordinary questions and every competent broker has an immediate answer.
